The story

I started where design meets code: designing and hand‑coding email templates at Apsis, then two years as a web developer at Aller Media, one of the Nordics’ biggest publishers, where A/B testing and analytics became habit.

Product management came next, at CDON — a major Nordic marketplace — first as a product manager, then as Head of Product for the consumer side, leading six cross‑functional teams and thirty‑odd people. My favourite win from that time is small and telling: I led the team that redesigned the product detail page and lifted conversion by around ten per cent, shipped against a Black Friday deadline.

In 2023, two founders with a concept — wine, sold the way it should be — asked me to join them and build it. Free Grape Society connects independent European winemakers directly with Swedish restaurants and consumers, shipped cellar to door, with Wine Experts curating along the way. I co‑founded, and I lead product and tech: strategy, UX, delivery, and the hard calls in between.

I still build. I was the marketplace’s most senior developer for a long stretch, and I work AI‑native — most recently leading the initiative that used LLMs to categorise and enrich our entire product catalogue. Design, code, and product aren’t three careers for me. They’re one loop, and I keep it closed.

Shipped

Three pieces of work from Free Grape Society, each the same shape: a real problem, a shipped answer, a measured result.

The pivot: one channel to two

We launched selling to restaurants, planning to scale through Sweden’s alcohol monopoly. Regulation closed that route almost entirely — while research kept pointing at real consumer demand.

I led the expansion of the sales model to consumers: dual pricing that stayed simple for buyers and for operations, coordinated across tech, design and marketing so the new channel wouldn’t undercut the old one, with Expert curation kept at the centre of the consumer experience.

B2C became the growth engine and now drives the majority of our revenue — a second, more resilient stream where there had been one.

Mixboxes

Consumers don’t want six bottles of the same wine, and that’s exactly what a standard case forced on them. You could see it in conversion.

I introduced Mixboxes — cases of six different wines, curated by the winemakers themselves — and built the feature with the tech team, instrumented from day one.

Purchase volume on mix‑enabled products roughly tripled, and producers gained a creative surface that deepened their engagement with the platform.

Guest checkout

After the pivot, checkout still demanded an account. Reasonable for a restaurant ordering monthly; real friction for someone buying their first case of wine.

I analysed the drop‑off, found registration was the sticking point, and designed a guest flow that still captured everything fulfilment and marketing consent actually needed.

Purchase volume through the flow more than doubled, and checkout abandonment fell materially for first‑time buyers.

How I think

Put people at the centre. Everything I ship has to answer three questions, and I ask them in order:

  1. What people‑problem are we solving?
  2. How do we know it’s a real problem?
  3. How will we know we’ve solved it?

The numbers matter — I instrument everything and let experiments settle arguments — but they exist to answer these questions, not to replace them.
