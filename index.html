I still build, these days with AI in the loop — for research, for prototyping, and in production, where we used LLMs to categorise and enrich the entire product catalogue. Design, code, and product aren’t three careers for me. They’re one loop, and I keep it closed.

Shipped

Three pieces of work from Free Grape Society.

The pivot: one channel to two

Problem
We launched selling to restaurants, planning to scale through Sweden’s alcohol monopoly. Regulation closed that route almost entirely — while research kept pointing at real consumer demand.
What I did
I led the expansion to consumers: dual pricing that stayed simple for buyers and for operations, coordinated across tech, design and marketing so the new channel wouldn’t undercut the old one — with Expert curation kept at the centre.
Impact
B2C became the primary revenue stream. It now drives the majority of revenue — a second, more resilient channel where there had been one.

Mixboxes

Problem
Consumers don’t want six bottles of the same wine — exactly what a standard case forced on them. You could see it in conversion.
What I did
I introduced Mixboxes — cases of six different wines, curated by the winemakers themselves — and built the feature with the tech team, instrumented from day one.
Impact
Purchase volume roughly tripled on mix‑enabled products, and producers gained a creative surface that deepened their engagement.

Guest checkout

Problem
After the pivot, checkout still demanded an account. Reasonable for a restaurant ordering monthly; real friction for someone buying their first case of wine.
What I did
I analysed the drop‑off, found registration was the sticking point, and designed a guest flow that still captured what fulfilment and marketing consent needed.
Impact
Purchase volume more than doubled through the flow; checkout abandonment fell materially for first‑time buyers.

What people hire me for

  • Turning ambiguity into shipped product. When the plan collapses — as ours did against the alcohol monopoly — I find the next viable model and get it live.
  • Bridging product and engineering. I’ve been the most senior developer in the room and the head of product. I translate between the two without losing anything in either direction.
  • Building on evidence. I instrument what I ship, let experiments settle arguments, and use AI as a working tool — research, prototypes, production pipelines — not a slide in a deck.

How I think

Product, design and engineering work best as one conversation, and I’ve sat in all three seats — so customer insight and technical reality reach decisions together, not in sequence. Everything I ship still has to answer three questions, in order:

  1. What people‑problem are we solving?
  2. How do we know it’s a real problem?
  3. How will we know we’ve solved it?

Measurement supports judgement; it doesn’t replace it. The numbers are there to answer these questions — never to stand in for them.
